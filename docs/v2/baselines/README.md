# Baselines

`latency-macos.json` is the stored latency baseline: what v1's hooks take, by the benchmark's scenarios, before any hook changes. Until the results store holds it, it is this file. The gate itself never reads it: the gate compares two binaries on one machine in one run (`scripts/benchmark.py --compare OLD NEW`). The file is the record a later change is read against.

## Refreshing it

The `latency` job of `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, on macOS and apart from the tests, runs the gate on every push to `main` and every pull request, and saves an artifact named `latency` that holds two files:

- `gate.json`: the verdict, every figure's comparison, each run's full report, the ceilings, the scenario each figure comes from, and every sample, by role and run, unrounded.
- `latency-macos.json`: the change's own binary's figures over its runs, `new` and `new_again`, in the format below.

To refresh the baseline, open the run of the commit to take it from (a push to `main`), download its `latency` artifact, check that `gate.json` says `"verdict": "pass"`, and copy its `latency-macos.json` over this folder's, unchanged. A pass means the machine was quiet enough: every figure's controls stayed within the gate's rule. The load average the file records says little on the CI machine, where it reads about 15 on 3 CPUs at the start of a job that has only just built two binaries. Commit it with the run's link in the message. The file names its commit, so the run can be found again.

The file in this folder now comes from the `latency` job's run of commit 84f0fc7 on `main`, the first in which the gate ran in a job of its own, and its verdict was pass: v1's hooks as they stand before any package of the v2 build has changed one. (The commit changed only the CI workflow and documents, so the two binaries it compared were alike.) The first file here was measured on Linux, in a builder's cloud session, to show the format; this one replaces it.

## Format

One JSON object:

| Key | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `v` | the format's version, 1 |
| `measured_on` | the system the figures were taken on, as Python's `platform.system()` names it, lower case: `darwin` on the macOS job |
| `machine` | the processor's architecture and its count of CPUs |
| `user` | `not root`, or `root`, as which the benchmark ran: as root eva-md keeps no identity cache, so every hook runs git three times, unlike any real install |
| `load_average_at_start` | the one-minute load average when the gate started |
| `commit` | the commit the measured binary was built from (`--commit`; empty when not given) |
| `runs` | how many runs of the binary the figures pool: both its roles, twice the gate's `--rounds` |
| `p95` | how the 95th percentile is taken: by the nearest rank, process start included |
| `taken_at` | when, in UTC |
| `metrics` | for each hook figure the gate compares, by its name: `n` (samples), `p95_ms`, `median_ms` (both by the nearest rank) and `target_ms`, the hook's target in the benchmark, or `null` |
| `counts` | figures that are counts, from the first run: the processes each host's launch path starts (`hook_launch_*_processes`), and the hooks run and processes started by a turn of 50 tool calls (`turn_*_hooks`, `turn_*_processes`) |
| `per_run` | figures with one value a run, listed by run: the milliseconds a turn of 50 tool calls summed over all its hooks and over the ones the host waits for (`turn_*_ms_summed`, `turn_*_blocking_ms_summed`), and the brief built by command at the real install's shape (`real_install_brief_command_median_ms`, `real_install_brief_command_p95_ms`) |

The metrics' names are the benchmark's report keys without `_p95_ms`, such as `capture` for `capture_p95_ms`. A process count depends on the machine: as root eva-md keeps no identity cache, so every hook then runs git three times.

## The gate's rule

Each round runs every scenario on each binary twice, as four roles: `old`, `new`, `old_again` and `new_again`, old and new alternating, and every other round in reverse (`--rounds`, 2 by default, so eight runs, four of each binary). The gate keeps each role's samples by run. All the old binary's runs together are compared with all the new one's, by the lower bound of the bootstrap interval (95%, 2,000 draws, a fixed seed) of the ratio of the new binary's p95 to the old one's. The p95 of a resample is one of the sorted samples, and its rank is drawn directly, from the law of the 95th percentile of that many uniform draws, so no resample is built: the bound is the one resampling gives, the same for the same samples in any order, and quick to work out.

The controls are splits of the runs into two sides that each hold half of the old binary's runs and half of the new one's: 18 at two rounds, among them the two splits of whole roles, `old`+`new` against `old_again`+`new_again` and `old`+`new_again` against `old_again`+`new`. With more rounds the controls are a seeded sample of at most 36 splits that always holds those two. Each side holds as many runs of each binary, so only what the machine did between runs is left between the sides. Load that falls on some runs and not others puts as much between the sides of some split as between the two binaries: load on one run of `new` and one of `new_again` is seen by the 6 splits that put both on one side, though neither split of whole roles can see it. A figure's noise is the 0.89 quantile, by nearest rank, of its controls' lower bounds, both ways, and at least 1: the fourth largest of 36 at two rounds, the eighth largest of 72 at three.

A change cancels out of the controls too, unless it is large. When the new binary is much slower, each side's p95 comes from its two runs of the new binary alone, so the controls measure two of its runs against two, on a quarter of the samples, and say the machine was noisier than it was: as merged, the gate caught a tripled hook less often than a rise of 20%. So when the binaries' typical runs (the lower median of each binary's runs' medians, which load on half of a binary's runs or fewer leaves on a run the load missed) differ by more than 1.15, the controls first bring the new binary's samples to 1.15 times the old one's, and work out the bounds on those. They bring it to 1.15 and not to 1: a gap between two unchanged binaries larger than that comes from runs that differ a lot, and it should still show in the controls.

Why the fourth largest bound: the largest of 36 is high whenever any one split is, and with it an unchanged binary was not judged 45 times in 100 on a quiet machine in the simulation below; the fourth still sees load on one run of each of the new binary's roles, whose 6 splits are well above it.

A figure is judged only with at least 100 samples on each binary's side. In the gate's runs, and only there, every hook a scenario times fewer than 34 times a run is timed 34 times (the chat-start figures, `code_brief`, `code_prompt`, `capture_with_spawn`, `session_end` and `real_install_startup`), so that a binary's three runs still make 100 when one of its four lost the scenario; and one chat start is timed and thrown away before the first chat-start figure's, since a run's first chat start takes about twice as long as the rest and, among 100 samples, would sit at the p95. Those extra chat starts' files are removed before the chat starts with chats a program ran, which a brief reads beyond its 64 newest files: 34 of them, and 34 of those, would push the person's chat out of the 64, as the plain benchmark's 10 never do. The plain benchmark's report and its sample counts are as they were. A figure with fewer than 100 samples on a side is listed as having too few: it neither fails nor passes. When it is short only because a scenario failed in some runs (the scenario that gives it failed, and each run, given as many samples as its fullest, would have made 100), the gate is not judged, with a line that says to run it again; when it is short by design, as at `--rounds 1`, it is only listed. `gate.json` records the scenario each figure comes from, so a figure the new binary gave no samples is excused only when the new binary lost that figure's own scenario in every run.

A judged figure whose noise is above 1.15 is too noisy to judge: it cannot fail. Two or more such figures say the machine was too noisy, and stop the whole verdict. So does one of a binary's runs being more than 1.15 times as quick as its next, by the median over the judged figures of each run's median against that figure's typical run: load on all of a binary's runs but one is what no control can tell from a change, since every split has a loaded run of that binary on each side, but the one run the load missed shows in every figure. Only the runs themselves call the machine noisy. Otherwise a figure fails when its lower bound is above 1.15 times its noise, or when it passes its ceiling: the lower bound of the interval of the new binary's p95 is above the ceiling and the old binary's p95 is not, so a figure at its ceiling by sampling alone does not fail. The ceiling is the target times `--ceiling-factor`: 2 on the CI machine, the default, and 1 on the reference Mac at a release; 0 turns the ceilings off. The controls have no ceiling: a fixed ceiling says where a binary sits, not how noisy the machine is. A figure whose lower bound is above 1.15 but not above 1.15 times its noise is unsettled. A figure too noisy to judge, or unsettled, stops nothing: another figure that failed still fails the gate, and without one the gate is not judged.

A scenario that fails in a run, as when load runs a hook past its time limit, leaves that run's samples of it out and counts against the binary that failed it. A hook still running after 4 seconds is killed with its whole process group, on Codex's launch path too, where the shell runs the binary as its child. The new binary failing a scenario in at least three quarters of its runs more than the old one (3 of 4 at two rounds) fails the gate, as a hook that hangs or fails would. Half of its runs more (2 of 4) makes the verdict `not judged`, with a line that says to run the gate again: load can lose a scenario that often. Fewer decides nothing. The verdict is the first of these that applies:

1. `could not run`, exit 2: a scenario failed in every run of both binaries, or the gate itself stopped on an error.
2. `fail`, exit 1: the new binary failed a scenario in three quarters of its runs more than the old one, or gave no samples of a figure the old one gave samples of, without losing that figure's scenario in every run.
3. `not judged`, exit 0: two or more judged figures were too noisy to judge, or one run of a binary was more than 1.15 times as quick as its next.
4. `fail`, exit 1: a figure failed.
5. `not judged`, exit 0: a figure was too noisy to judge or unsettled, a scenario was lost in half the new binary's runs more, or a figure had too few samples because a scenario failed in some runs.
6. `pass`, exit 0.

A figure the old binary gave no samples, as one added with the change, is listed as not compared. `gate.json` keeps every sample by role and run, unrounded, with the ceilings and the scenario of each figure, so that `redecide` in `scripts/bench/core.py` works the verdict out again exactly. The step is advisory until an unchanged binary has passed it twenty times in twenty; then the lead removes `continue-on-error` from it.

This rule differs from the card's rulings in three places, each for a reviewer's measurement. A single figure too noisy to judge no longer stops the whole verdict, as ruling 2 had it: two do, or the quick run above (on the saved gates' samples one noisy figure stopped about one gate in six, and with it a real rise was caught too seldom). The controls bring a large change to 1.15 first, which ruling 1 did not say. And a figure short of 100 only because a scenario failed in some runs leaves the gate not judged, where ruling 3 had it stop nothing.

### The rule measured

`python3 -I scripts/bench/sim_gate.py` feeds the rule gates with the gate's own sample counts, under a load and with a change to the new binary, 300 seeds a case, and prints the share of fail, pass and not judged. It takes minutes, and is run by hand. It has two bases. The made-up one has the shapes the proof's samples have (a long right tail, a few slow outliers, runs whose medians differ by 3.3% to 7.5% on a quiet machine, which is the median over the saved gates), and lets that spread differ from gate to gate (a lognormal factor of standard deviation 0.4 on each figure's spread) and from run to run the share of samples in the tail, so that its runs' p95s differ about as much as the saved gates' do. The saved base (`--gates`) takes the 18 of the proof's 20 gates of an unchanged binary that lost no run, deals one gate's eight runs to the four roles at random, and puts the load and the change on them; its chat-start figures had 10 samples a run then, so they are too few to judge there, and only the other figures are. The loads: none; light bursts (about one a run, a twentieth of a run long, 1.2 to 1.6 times slower); heavy bursts (three a run, a tenth of a run long, 1.5 to 3 times); whole runs (each run loaded or not on its own, a quarter of them, 1.2 to 1.8 times); a drift (the machine half as slow again by the end); one run of `new` and one of `new_again` loaded, 1.2 to 2 times; and three or all four of the new binary's runs loaded and none of the old one's, 1.2 to 2 times.

On the same base samples a seed, on a quiet machine, every hook 20%, 30% and 50% slower, and two and three times as slow (`--monotonic`): on the made-up base, at 100 seeds, they failed 96, 97, 97, 97 and 97 times in 100, and in no seed did a larger rise not fail where a smaller one failed; on the saved gates' base, at 150 seeds, 87.3, 92.7, 92.7, 92.7 and 92.7 times in 100, and in 2 seeds of 150 a rise of 20% failed where the larger ones were not judged. Those 2 are what bringing a large change to 1.15, not to 1, leaves: in those gates the rise of 20% left two figures' typical runs about 1.11 times the old ones', within 1.15, so the controls took them as they were, while the larger rises were brought to 1.15, further apart, and those two figures' controls then just passed 1.15. As merged, a hook twice as slow was caught 74 times in 100 where a rise of 20% was caught 87 times, by its reviewer's model.

Against the card's aims, on the made-up base: an unchanged binary failed at most 0.7 times in 100 under every load, a quiet machine and load on one run of each of the new binary's roles among them (aim: at most 1); it was not judged 11.3 times in 100 on a quiet machine (aim: at most 5); a rise of 30% in every hook failed 97 times in 100 (aim: at least 85), one of 20% 96.3 (aim: at least 70), and 20 ms added to capture alone 99.3 (aim: at least 95). On the saved gates' base: an unchanged binary failed at most 0.7 times in 100 under every load; it was not judged 14.3 times in 100 on a quiet machine; a rise of 30% failed 94.3 times in 100, one of 20% 87, and 20 ms added to capture 100. Every aim is reached on both bases but one, which is not: an unchanged binary on a quiet machine is not judged about one time in eight, not one in twenty. What leaves it not judged is a figure whose own runs differ by more than 1.15 between splits: on the saved gates `capture_with_spawn` and `transcript_capture`, whose runs' p95s differ by 8% to 11% in a typical gate. Calling the machine noisy only from two figures made a rise caught far more often, but cannot lower this: a figure too noisy to judge might hide a rise of its own, so with nothing else failing the gate is not judged. A lower quantile barely lowers it (the sixth largest bound gave 8.7 and 14.7 in 100 on the two bases, at 150 seeds, where the fourth gave 10 and 15.3 in the same trial), and the largest raises it to 45 in 100. Reaching 5 would take more runs a gate, or passing a figure too noisy to judge, which would let a rise that load hid in that one figure through.

What the gate cannot tell. Load on all four of the new binary's runs and none of the old one's looks to every control exactly like a change: it fails 97 times in 100 on the made-up base, as a change would. Load on three of them is caught by the quick run left: it fails 0.3 times in 100, and is not judged the rest, where without that check the rule failed it 93 times in 100 (at 150 seeds), since no control sees it and two figures too noisy to judge rarely do. Under load the gate is mostly not judged, and a regression under load is too: under light bursts a rise of 30% in every hook failed 19.7 times in 100 and 20 ms added to capture 15.7, and with whole runs loaded 11.3 and 10.7, and the rest were not judged; a binary unchanged under the same loads failed at most 0.3 times in 100. So a `not judged` on CI, where the load reads about 15 on 3 CPUs, is not a pass: the gate is to be run again.

The rule as it was merged first, measured by its reviewers: on the proof's samples an unchanged binary failed 4.7, 9.3 and 5 times in 100 under light bursts, heavy bursts and whole runs loaded, a rise of 20% failed 33 times in 100 and one of 30% 43.5, and two runs of a scenario lost failed the build. The rule of the first round of this card (one noisy figure stopping the verdict, controls of the raw samples, 25 chat starts a run) was measured on a made-up base without the spread from gate to gate: every aim met there. On the saved gates' samples it was not judged on an unchanged binary 16 to 19 times in 100, and caught a rise of 20% 65 times in 100, one of 30% 67 times and 20 ms added to capture 84 to 87 times; and a larger rise was caught less often than a smaller one (a tripled hook not judged where a rise of 20% failed), since the change itself made the controls noisy and one noisy figure stopped the verdict.

The 21 gates of that proof, 20 of an unchanged binary and one with 20 ms added to capture, worked out again by this rule from their samples by run (`sim_gate.py --rework`): the unchanged ones passed 16 times and were not judged 4 times, and never failed; the one with 20 ms added failed, on 12 figures. Of the 4, `same-3` and `same-10` had one figure too noisy to judge (`transcript_capture`, `capture_with_spawn`) and `same-20` two (`transcript_capture` and `turn_claude_code_tool`); `same-19` and `same-20` each lost the scenario of the padded inbox in one run, which left 3 of its figures (`capture_with_spawn`, `code_prompt` and `session_end`, at 30 and 25 a run then) short of 100 a side, so the gate was not judged, to be run again. Their chat-start figures had 10 samples a run then: 5 figures were listed as having too few samples in each gate, and 8 in `same-19` and `same-20`.
